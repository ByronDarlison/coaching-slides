Owner's Outcome Guardrails

The edges you protect on the way to the goal.

  • What failure looks like. The picture of this business failing you even if the numbers hit. Not what you are afraid of. What you refuse to walk into on purpose.
  • Non-Negotiables. The rules you will not break even if it costs you money or growth. Time, health, family, the kind of work you do, the kind of people you work with. If you have broken one before, it is not a non-negotiable.
  • Cash Floor. The number below which you start making decisions from anxiety instead of clarity. Not the number that sounds reasonable. The real one.
  • Home Alignment. If you have a partner, do they know what you want from this business, and do they agree? Misalignment at home will override everything else you build.

Key Function Flow Map (KFFM)

Walk through the draft you brought from onboarding. First pass. We will coach again at M2.

  • Walk the flow from lead to cash. Does it reflect how money actually moves?
  • Confirm the 3 to 5 key functions. Anything missing or doubled up?
  • Sharpen the widgets: countable things, not dollars
  • Color code green, yellow, red. What is winning, what is losing?
  • This is the master. The FAC and FOC derive from it.

Functional Accountability Chart (FAC)

Walk through the draft you brought. First pass. We will coach again at M2.

  • For each function on the KFFM: who owns it, and what one number tells you it is winning or losing
  • The test: can the owner actually move the number by changing how they run the function?
  • Set green, yellow, red thresholds for each critical number
  • The critical number comes from the KFFM widget, not the financial report

Functional Organization Chart (FOC)

Function first. People second. Level 2 typically mirrors the key functions you named on your KFFM. Level 3 and below decomposes the work that makes each one actually happen. Names get placed against the structure last.

  • Walk through the draft you brought. Function first, people second.
  • Identify where the gaps are: open roles and future functions
  • One person can appear in multiple boxes. Where is the overload?
  • Functions with subfunctions show that hierarchy

Profit/X

The one unit of measurement that captures how the company makes money: profit per customer, per transaction, per subscription.

  • Walk through your Profit/X. Is it the single denominator that captures how the company makes money?
  • The obvious billing unit is often wrong. X usually emerges from the widgets on the KFFM.
  • Once identified, every function aligns around one shared measure
