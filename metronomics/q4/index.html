10 comp-story

A team builds cohesion in five levels, from trust up.

Each level rests on the one below it. Your Five Dysfunctions assessment scored each level.

darlison.com

This is the model from The Five Dysfunctions of a Team. The book was the Q2 reading. The team assessment was Q3 homework.

The five levels, from the bottom: trust, conflict, commitment, accountability, results. A team that does not trust each other will not have honest conflict. Without honest conflict nobody truly commits. Without commitment nobody holds each other accountable. Without accountability the team’s results come second to each person’s own.

The assessment scores each level. High is 4 and above. Medium is 3 to 3.99. Low is under 3.

Do the assessment at least once a year. Use the scores to choose where the team works on its cohesiveness next. Keep them as the benchmark for next year.

If the team has not taken the assessment, assign it with a date and move to the Function Chart.

41 comp-story

Build your Activity Fit Map in four steps.

To create your white space, you must invest in some things and give up others. Those are your activities.

darlison.com

Start from the white space the team just agreed on. To create it, the company must invest in some things and give up others. Those are the activities.

Walk the four steps. The team brainstorms every activity, groups them and names each group. It keeps 3 to 5. Those are the differentiators. Then it draws the lines.

This is the Activity Fit Map. The idea is Michael Porter’s: strategy is the set of activities a company does differently from its competitors. Shannon Susko builds it from the ground up.

Do not start from the attributes at the top of the 3HAG line and work down. That gives generic names.

The lines are the point. Differentiators that depend on each other are hard to copy. With no lines, the team has a list of capabilities.

The end is a map of what the company must do to create its 3HAG. The 3 to 5 differentiators become the swim lanes: what must get done, quarter by quarter, over the next three years.

Draw the lines only between the 3 to 5 the team kept. A line joins two differentiators. It does not join two activities.

A line means one depends on the other. Start with the most-connected differentiator. For each of the others, ask: if we cannot do that one, can we win with this one? Draw the line when the team agrees.

A line has no arrow. Say which one depends on which. A differentiator can depend on more than one other.

Write the reason on the line. This is Darlison practice, not Shannon’s. It makes the team say why.

All joined: the differentiators work as one set. A competitor who copies one piece gets little.

None joined: the team has a list of capabilities. It is rare to get this far and find that.
