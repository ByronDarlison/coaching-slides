17 comp-story

Every function owner has a scorecard, reviewed every 90 days.

What: one page per function, saying what success looks like.

Why: everyone knows what they own and how we measure it.

How often: every 90 days, scored 0, 1 or 2.

Who: the owner, then their team lead. Then they compare.

darlison.com

The scores: 0 below, 1 meets, 2 exceeds. The owner scores first, then their team lead, then they talk about the differences.

A scorecard is not a job description or a list of priorities. It belongs to a function on the KFFM.

The review is a two-way conversation. The learning is where the two scores differ. At the end, the team member gives their leader feedback.

First time, keep it to the team member and their team lead. Metronome runs the reviews.

55 comp-decision

Pick the priorities that contribute most to success.

Builds

What does it build or move?

A 3HAG capability, or a KFFM lever that brings cash in faster.

Impact

How much will it move a critical number?

Name the number and the target. If it barely moves, think twice.

Speed

How soon will we see it?

Name the date the measure of success will be achieved by. If it takes a long time to show, think twice.

Owner

Who can own it and succeed?

One person, with the capacity and the skills to deliver it.

Cost

What will it cost?

Money, time and attention. Choose the most impact for the least cost.

Risk

What could go wrong?

Think through the second-order consequences.

darlison.com

Ask these six questions of every candidate. Keep the few that contribute most: ideally 3, up to 5. A routine task list is not a set of company priorities.

Builds: fix the cause, not the symptom. A quarterly priority advances a 1HAG priority.

Impact and speed are the two warning signs. If the impact takes a long time to show, or it barely moves a critical number, think twice. There can be good reasons to go ahead anyway, but weigh both every time.

Owner: can one person be accountable? Do they have the time, given what they already carry? Do they have the skills, or the support, to succeed? If no one fits, the priority is not ready, or the first step is to find the owner.

Risk: ask how badly we could mess this up.

Once chosen, ask an owner to claim each priority aloud and agree the done-by date, the measure and the date it will be achieved by. If two priorities compete for the same people or cash, agree which one wins. The next slide shows why done and success are different dates.

A wish with a number on it

Financial statements

Two to ten weeks late

Finance alone

Answers for cash it can’t move

69 comp-story

Every month has three numbers.

darlison.com

Approved is the plan the team locks at annual planning. It does not change during the year.

Actual replaces the plan as each month closes. Finance enters it.

The model calculates Rolling: Actual to date plus forecasted Approved. Any difference carries forward into every later month.

Never rewrite Approved mid-year. The gap between Rolling and Approved is information, not embarrassment.
