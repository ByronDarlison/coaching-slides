Four tries on my own fell short. The fifth added a coach, a system and one customer.

1996: more money, same founder doing everything. 2001: an LED sign maker took a stake, same problem, no system. 2004: a new leader with no system to run. 2017: Scaling Up and Topgrading without a coach. 2018: one customer, K-12 schools in North America, and a year of cash in the bank by 2020.

22 comp-story

KFFM: Your Path to Success

iProfit / XJim Collins’s economic engine (Good to Great): the one profit ratio that, if you raised it steadily over time, would do the most to drive your economics. Here, profit per subscription.
darlison.com

Point each lever at the example map. Once the team can see the flow, these are the ways to improve it.

Byron: the map's quantities, conversion rates, price, timing, capacity and cost are the levers the owner uses to manage cash.

Timing is how long one widget takes to get through. Capacity is how many widgets a function can handle in a month. A function can be fast and still too small.

23 comp-story

KFFM: Your AI Roadmap

iProfit / XJim Collins’s economic engine (Good to Great): the one profit ratio that, if you raised it steadily over time, would do the most to drive your economics. Here, profit per subscription.
darlison.com

Every box on the map already states what it receives, what it produces, its critical number and its days. Those are the tests for whether an AI agent could do that work.

If a person activates 10 subscriptions a day and an agent handles 100, around the clock, days to cash drops and volume rises for every function downstream.

Do not automate work that depends on judgment, relationships or trust. The critical number will punish a bad automation quickly.

Keep the map current: it is the roadmap for where AI goes next.
