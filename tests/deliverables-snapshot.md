## Deliverable Details

### Owner's Outcome

**Category:** Cultural  
**Introduced:** Kick-Off  
**Reference:** https://www.darlison.com/are-you-getting-what-you-want/  
**AI Prompt:** https://www.darlison.com/owners-outcome-prompt/  

What the owner personally wants the business to deliver -- by when, measured by what. Defined at the start and reviewed every quarter. Every business decision should move the owner closer to this outcome.

### KFFM

**Category:** Human  
**Introduced:** Kick-Off  
**Reference:** https://www.darlison.com/how-your-company-makes-money/  
**AI Prompt:** https://www.darlison.com/kffm-tools/  

A map showing how work flows through the company -- from acquiring a customer to getting paid. The things that flow through each function (leads, orders, deliveries) are called widgets. Level 1 is the company-wide view. Level 2 zooms into each function. Level 3+ goes deeper. It takes a full year to really map this out. The process is iterative and never truly finished.

### Functional Accountability Chart

**Category:** Human  
**Introduced:** Kick-Off  
**Reference:** https://www.darlison.com/how-your-company-makes-money/  
**AI Prompt:** https://www.darlison.com/kffm-tools/  

Assigns every function an owner and a critical number -- the one metric that tells you if that function is winning or losing. Built alongside the KFFM. Gets sharper as the team gets clearer on their widgets.

### Functional Organization Chart (FOC)

**Category:** Human  
**Introduced:** Kick-Off  
**Reference:** https://www.darlison.com/how-your-company-makes-money/  
**AI Prompt:** https://www.darlison.com/kffm-tools/  

A chart showing every function the company needs (Sales, Product, Finance, etc.), who owns each one, and where the gaps are. Not an org chart by title -- a map by function. Updated every meeting as the company evolves.

### Profit/X

**Category:** Cultural  
**Introduced:** Kick-Off  
**Reference:** https://www.darlison.com/how-your-company-makes-money/  
**AI Prompt:** https://www.darlison.com/kffm-tools/  

The one unit of measurement that best captures how the company makes money -- profit per customer, per transaction, per subscription. Gutted out at the Kick-Off from the KFFM. Gets clearer as the team understands what flows through the business.

### Core Purpose

**Category:** Cultural  
**Introduced:** Kick-Off  
**Reference:** https://www.darlison.com/why-does-your-company-exist/  
**AI Prompt:** https://www.darlison.com/core-purpose-prompt/  

Why the company exists beyond making money. The team writes their first version -- good enough, not perfect. Confirmed every meeting. Validated once a year.

### BHAG

**Category:** Cultural  
**Introduced:** Kick-Off  
**Reference:** https://www.darlison.com/why-does-your-company-exist/  
**AI Prompt:** https://www.darlison.com/core-purpose-prompt/  

A 10-to-30-year goal with no numbers -- just a destination the whole team can believe in and align to. Confirmed every meeting. Deepened annually.

### Meeting Cadence

**Category:** Execution  
**Introduced:** Kick-Off  
**Reference:** https://www.darlison.com/the-eight-meetings-that-run-your-company/  
**AI Prompt:** https://www.darlison.com/meeting-cadence-assessment/  

Eight meetings at six frequencies: daily huddle (15 min), weekly tactical (1 hr), weekly CEO email, monthly leadership (4 hrs), monthly town hall (1 hr), weekly 1-on-1s, quarterly planning, and 90-day coaching reviews. Includes the Monthly Forecast Review. Set once. Runs continuously.

### A-Player Team Assessment

**Category:** Human  
**Introduced:** Kick-Off  
**Reference:** https://www.darlison.com/a-player-team-assessment/  
**AI Prompt:** https://www.darlison.com/a-player-team-assessment-prompt/  

A graph plotting every team member on two axes: values alignment and performance. First version is gut-feel alongside Core Values discovery. Once values are confirmed, the full leadership team runs the assessment with the values axis in place. Reviewed every meeting after.

### Market Map

**Category:** Strategy  
**Introduced:** Kick-Off  

A visual map of every player in your market -- competitors, substitutes, adjacent companies -- plotted by relevant dimensions (price, features, geography). Shows where the open space is. Reviewed every meeting.

### 3HAG

**Category:** Strategy  
**Introduced:** Kick-Off  
**Reference:** https://www.darlison.com/where-are-you-going/  
**AI Prompt:** https://www.darlison.com/planning-cascade-prompt/  

A 3-Year Highly Achievable Goal -- fiscal targets, key capabilities, and the widgets needed to get there. The team writes their first version. Validated every meeting. Rebuilt from scratch at the annual to force fresh thinking.

### 1HAG

**Category:** Execution  
**Introduced:** Kick-Off  
**Reference:** https://www.darlison.com/where-are-you-going/  
**AI Prompt:** https://www.darlison.com/planning-cascade-prompt/  

The 1-Year Highly Achievable Goal -- the annual plan broken into priorities, metrics, and owners. Aligns to the 3HAG. Rebuilt at each annual meeting.

### QHAG + 13-Week Sprint Lanes

**Category:** Execution  
**Introduced:** Kick-Off  
**Reference:** https://www.darlison.com/where-are-you-going/  
**AI Prompt:** https://www.darlison.com/planning-cascade-prompt/  

The 90-day plan -- 3-5 corporate priorities for the quarter, each with metrics and an owner. Sprint Lanes break each priority into weekly milestones across 13 weeks so the team can see if they're on track every week, not just at the end. Rebuilt at every quarterly boundary.

### Core Values

**Category:** Cultural  
**Introduced:** Q1  
**Reference:** https://www.darlison.com/how-to-discover-your-companys-values-using-ai  
**AI Prompt:** https://www.darlison.com/values-discovery-prompt/  

The 3-5 non-negotiable behaviors the team expects of each other. Not aspirational -- descriptive of how the best people already act. Discovery begins at M4 using a structured process (Mission to Mars). Confirmed and locked. Reviewed every meeting after.

### Function Scorecards

**Category:** Human  
**Introduced:** Q1  
**Reference:** https://www.darlison.com/scorecards/  
**AI Prompt:** https://www.darlison.com/scorecards-tools/  

A one-page view of what success looks like for each function: the role's purpose, key accountabilities, critical numbers, and the values expected. The CEO creates the first one (Head of Company) at M5. All functions get scorecards at Q4 annual. Scoreboard Day is the weekly rhythm for reviewing scorecard numbers and turning gaps into fixes.

### Core Customer Analysis

**Category:** Strategy  
**Introduced:** Q3  
**Reference:** https://www.darlison.com/who-is-your-most-valuable-customer/  
**AI Prompt:** https://www.darlison.com/core-customer-discovery-prompt/  

A detailed profile of the one customer type who buys at a profit -- not every customer, just the most valuable one. Introduced after the Market Map gives the team context on the competitive landscape. Identifies who they are, their top 3 needs, and the benefits you offer to serve those needs.

### 12-Month Widget-Based Forecast

**Category:** Cash  
**Introduced:** Q3  

A 12-month profit-and-loss forecast built on widgets (the things that flow through the company) instead of top-down revenue guesses. Finance builds the model. Leaders own the assumptions for their function's widgets.

### Attribution Map

**Category:** Strategy  
**Introduced:** Q4★  

A matrix showing how your company and competitors score on the attributes that matter most to your Core Customer. Reveals where you're differentiated and where you're not. Rebuilt from scratch at each annual to prevent anchoring on old assumptions.

### Activity Fit Map (Differentiators)

**Category:** Strategy  
**Introduced:** Q4★  

Michael Porter's framework showing the 3-5 capabilities that put your company in a unique position -- and the supporting activities required for each one. Built from the white space identified in the Attribution Map. Rebuilt at each annual.

### Swimlanes

**Category:** Strategy  
**Introduced:** Q4★  

A 12-quarter grid with one row per differentiating activity. Each row maps the major milestones needed to build that capability, quarter by quarter, for 3 years. Rebuilt from scratch at each annual alongside the Attribution Map and differentiators.

### Painted Picture

**Category:** Cultural  
**Introduced:** Q4★  

A vivid narrative describing what the company looks, feels, and operates like when the BHAG is achieved. Makes the long-term vision tangible and emotional for the whole team.

### Quarterly Coaching Reviews

**Category:** Coach Cascade  
**Introduced:** Q5  
**Reference:** https://www.darlison.com/coaching-framework/  

A 90-day two-way conversation based on the function scorecard -- not a top-down annual review. Both sides give and receive feedback. The CEO starts these with leaders once scorecards are in place. Later, leaders begin running the same reviews with their own team members -- the cascade.

### Positioning Statement (Moore)

**Category:** Strategy  
**Introduced:** Q6  

A structured statement from Geoffrey Moore's Crossing the Chasm: For [target customer] who [need], our [product] is a [category] that [key benefit] unlike [alternatives]. Takes all the strategy work and formats it into one confident, sayable statement.

### Value Proposition (Moore)

**Category:** Strategy  
**Introduced:** Q6  

The value exchange: what the customer gets and why it's worth what they pay. Paired with the Positioning Statement -- if the Positioning Statement says where you play, the Value Proposition says why you win.

### 36-Month Rolling Forecast

**Category:** Cash  
**Introduced:** Q6  

Extends the 12-month widget-based forecast all the way out to the 3HAG -- 36 months of widget and fiscal projections. Gives the team a line of sight from today's numbers to the 3-year goal. Reviewed monthly with the leadership team.

### Business Model Canvas

**Category:** Strategy  
**Introduced:** Q6  

Alex Osterwalder's framework mapping how the organization creates, delivers, and captures value. A strategic validation tool that uses the Positioning Statement and Value Proposition as a starting point.

### Skip-Level Reviews

**Category:** Coach Cascade  
**Introduced:** Q7  
**Reference:** https://www.darlison.com/skip-level-reviews/  

The CEO has a direct conversation with people one level below their leaders -- not to bypass the leader, but to surface how that leader is actually managing day to day. One conversation, one improvement, every 90 days.

### Porter's Five Forces

**Category:** Strategy  
**Introduced:** Q7  

Michael Porter's industry profitability analysis. Examines five competitive forces: rivalry, supplier power, buyer power, threat of substitution, and threat of new entry. A strategic validation tool.

### Flywheel

**Category:** Strategy  
**Introduced:** Q8★  

Jim Collins's concept: a self-reinforcing loop of activities where each step feeds the next, building momentum over time. The team maps their company's specific flywheel -- the sequence of actions that compound growth.

### eNPS

**Category:** Human  
**Introduced:** Q8★  

Employee Net Promoter Score -- a single-question pulse survey measuring team engagement. Simple, anonymous, and trackable over time.

### Brand Promise with Guarantee

**Category:** Strategy  
**Introduced:** Q9  

A public commitment to your Core Customer based on their top 3 needs -- what you promise to deliver, backed by a specific guarantee if you don't. Not made public until the team is confident they can deliver.

### BrandScript

**Category:** Strategy  
**Introduced:** Q9  

The StoryBrand framework applied to your company's messaging. Clarifies the story your company tells: the customer is the hero, your company is the guide, and the plan leads to success.

### Consumption Chain Mapping

**Category:** Strategy  
**Introduced:** Q10  

Maps the Core Customer's complete experience throughout their entire journey with your company. Validates strategy by analyzing how well execution serves the customer's full consumption experience.

### Secret Sauce

**Category:** Strategy  
**Introduced:** Q12★  

Your unique tenfold advantage -- the problem you solve that no one else in the market wants to solve. Most teams take months or years to identify it. Once found, never share it. Never stop looking for the next one.
